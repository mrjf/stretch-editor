Most of us were taught that a good morning is a fast one. Up early, inbox cleared before coffee, three wins logged by nine. The rush feels like progress, so we rarely stop to ask where it is taking us.

A slow morning works the other way around. You give the first hour to something that asks nothing of you: a walk, a pot of tea, a page of a book you are reading for no reason at all. Nothing gets crossed off, and that is the point.

What changes is not how much you do, but how you meet the day. People who start slowly report fewer snap decisions and less of that mid-afternoon crash. They have already had one unhurried hour, so the rest of the day has something to measure against.

So try it for a week. Push the first meeting back thirty minutes, leave the phone in another room, and let the kettle take as long as it takes. The work will still be there, and you may find you are better company for it.
